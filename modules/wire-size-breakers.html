How it works

  • AWG = American Wire Gauge. Smaller number = thicker wire = more amps it can carry safely.
  • Too many amps through a wire makes it heat up. Hot enough, the insulation melts and it can start a fire.
  • The breaker protects the wire, not the appliance. It shuts off when the amps go over its rating.
  • For loads that run for hours (heaters, AC), stay at or under 80% of the breaker.
  • Never put a bigger breaker on a smaller wire.

Challenges

1. Make the wire overheat without tripping the breaker. What did you change?
Answer: put a breaker that's bigger than the wire can handle (like a 30A breaker on 14 AWG) and set the load around 20–25A. The wire is over its 15A limit, but the breaker won't trip until 30A. That's the fire hazard.
2. On 12 AWG with a 20A breaker, what's the most a space heater (long-running load) should draw?
Answer: 16A, which is 80% of 20A. That's 1,920 watts. Slide past 16A and the status turns yellow.
3. An 1,800W hair dryer and a 600W TV share a 14 AWG / 15A circuit. What happens?
Answer: 2,400W ÷ 120V = 20A. That's over the 15A breaker, so it trips after a few seconds. The wire was protected because the breaker matches the wire.
